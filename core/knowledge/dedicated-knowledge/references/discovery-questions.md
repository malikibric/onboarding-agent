# Discovery Questions

For filling gaps when source material is thin, or for preparing the user to go interview a process owner.

Two principles that matter more than the question list itself:

**Ask about the last real instance, not the general case.** "Walk me through the last quote you sent" surfaces the actual process. "How do you handle quotes?" surfaces the idealized one. People describe their work as they believe it should be done and perform it differently, and the difference is exactly what an agent needs.

**Chase every hedge.** When someone says "usually", "it depends", or "normally", there is an unwritten rule underneath. Stop and dig: depends on what, specifically? What was the last exception?

---

## Scope

- If this agent could only do one thing well, what should it be?
- What part of this work do you most want off your plate?
- What is out of scope — what should it never touch, even if it looks related?

## Process

- Walk me through the last time you did this, start to finish.
- What starts it? What tells you it is finished?
- Where does it get stuck or slow down most often?
- What is the documented process, and where does the real one differ?
- Who touches it after you?

## Decisions

- Where in this process do you have to make a judgment call?
- What are you actually weighing when you make it?
- What would make you choose differently?
- Tell me about a time this went wrong. What did you miss?

## Authority

- What can you decide alone? What needs sign-off?
- Where is the line — at what number or what condition does it change?
- Who signs off, and what happens when they are unavailable?

## Data

- Where do you look to check that?
- Has that system ever disagreed with another one? Which did you trust?
- What do you keep in a spreadsheet or in your head because no system holds it properly?

## Vocabulary

- You said [term] — what does that mean here specifically?
- Is there anything a new hire consistently misunderstands in their first month?
- Any word that means something different here than it does elsewhere in the industry?

## Standards

- Can you send me three recent examples you were happy with?
- What makes one of these good rather than merely acceptable?
- What would a client or a colleague push back on?

## Limits

- What must never happen here?
- Anything regulated, contractual, or legally sensitive in this process?
- What would you want a new person to check with you about before acting?
- If this went wrong at 2am with nobody watching, what is the worst version?

---

## Reading transcripts

When you have a recording rather than a live conversation, mine it for these signals:

| Signal | What it usually indicates |
|---|---|
| Hedges — "usually", "it depends", "normally" | An unwritten decision rule |
| Unexplained nouns | A vocabulary entry the speaker is blind to |
| "Oh, and then I also..." | A process step missing from the documented version |
| War stories about past failures | A hard constraint or escalation trigger |
| "I'd have to check with..." | An authority boundary |
| "I just know" / "you get a feel for it" | Tacit knowledge — the hardest and most valuable thing to extract; push here |
