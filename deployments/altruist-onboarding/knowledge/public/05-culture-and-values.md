# Culture and Values — Public

> This content comes from the primary knowledge pack and is among the best-sourced material in the whole fact base. The old draft dropped it entirely in favour of unverified financial and regulatory specifics — a bad trade the audit flagged, and the reason this file exists.

## Core values

Altruist careers messaging highlights three core values: **Kindness, Brilliance, and Grit.** `[ALT-010]`

That is the sourced claim. What the agent may add is only the obvious reading — three words, presented by the company as what it looks for — with no invented elaboration about how they are measured, referenced in reviews, or used in hiring. None of that is known.

## Careers messaging

Careers messaging also emphasizes purpose, growth, equity/compensation, health benefits from day one, work-life integration, and inclusivity. `[ALT-011]`

**`ALT-011` is marked sensitive and carries a mandatory attribution.** It is careers-page *messaging*. It is not a description of an actual benefits package, and it must never be used to answer a question about what the hire is entitled to, when enrolment happens, or what anything costs. Those go to boundary `B-08`.

The distinction to hold: *"Altruist's careers page mentions health benefits from day one"* is answerable. *"You get health benefits from day one"* is not — it's an entitlement claim the agent cannot make.

## Offices

Publicly listed office locations include Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Dallas. `[ALT-012]`

Both sourced documents agree here. The old draft's "HQ Culver City, CA" is unsupported by either and is quarantined (`ALT-Q01`). Note the agent also does not know which office the hire is joining, whether roles are remote, or what any office is like inside — all internal.

## Not answerable

Actual culture, team norms, how people work, meeting habits, whether the values show up in practice, remote-work policy (`B-05`), and anything about specific people (`B-02`).

A new hire will reasonably ask "what's it actually like there?" The honest answer is that the agent has public messaging and nothing else, and that the people around them are the real source. That refusal is more useful than a synthesized vibe, and it models the habit the agent exists to teach.
